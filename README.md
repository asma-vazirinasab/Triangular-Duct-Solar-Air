# CFD Investigation of a Solar Air Heater with Square-Rib Roughness

## Project Overview

A solar air heater (SAH) is a device that utilizes solar radiation for heating applications. Its thermal performance can be improved by introducing artificial roughness elements, such as ribs, on the absorber plate to enhance heat transfer.

This project investigates the flow characteristics and heat transfer enhancement of a solar air heater with a triangular cross-section and square-shaped ribs using **Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)**. The study involves the design and numerical modelling of the solar air heater to evaluate the effects of rib geometry and Reynolds number on its thermal and hydraulic performance.

## Simulation Parameters

The effects of the following parameters were investigated:

* **Relative roughness pitch(P/e):** 5–13, across three configurations.
* **Relative roughness height (e/D):** 0.013–0.05, across three configurations.
* **Reynolds number (Re):** 3,900–17,900.

## Key Findings

* Introducing square ribs on the absorber plate enhanced heat transfer compared with a smooth surface.
* The maximum heat transfer enhancement was approximately **97%**, observed at (P/e = 10), (e/D = 0.05), and (Re = 17900).
* The maximum thermohydraulic performance parameter (TPP) was **1.97**, obtained under the same operating conditions.
* Correlations for the friction factor and Nusselt number were developed based on the simulation results.

## Objectives

* Investigate the effects of artificial roughness on heat transfer and flow behaviour.
* Evaluate the influence of rib geometry and Reynolds number on thermal and hydraulic performance.
* Assess thermohydraulic performance by considering heat transfer enhancement and frictional losses.
* Develop empirical correlations for the friction factor and Nusselt number based on the numerical results.
